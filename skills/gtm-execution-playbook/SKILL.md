# GTM Execution Playbook

`leverage-playbook` answers WHO makes a business big. This skill answers HOW to sell it week to week. Run leverage-playbook for strategy and this for execution. Keep them as separate docs.

Distilled from a founder-summit GTM session with operators who have taken B2B companies from zero to scale.

## Trigger

The user names a business and asks for outbound, an email sequence, cold email copy, a content or lead-magnet plan, an AEO or SEO plan, an event plan, a sales-hiring question, or "how do we go to market". Read everything provided about the business first. Ask only for what's missing: ICP, stage (pre or post $10M ARR), current channels and their reply rates.

## Step 1. Stage check

- Pre $10M ARR: founder-led. Do not recommend a VP Sales hire. The first sales leader is the BDR who sat in every meeting. Promote from inside.
- The flywheel test: the team hits 80% of quota without the founder, and the founder stays on the top 20% of deals by value. Until then, the founder sells.
- Before any product work, check pricing, packaging, churn and expansion. Those move revenue faster than features in the first $10M.
- PMF signal: similar customers renewing. Different kinds of customers churning means no PMF yet, whatever the pipeline says.
- Sales by process beats sales by heroics. Heroics are additive, never load-bearing.

## Step 2. Is the ICP flooded or untouched?

Ask: who else is emailing these people today?

- **Untouched** (builders, private distributors, trades, regional owners, athletes): email and text work. Go now and expect high reply rates.
- **Flooded** (tech, startups, VCs, PE, HR, anyone with an SDR aimed at them): email is a trigger-only channel. Lead with in-person, content, warm intros and trigger-based outreach.

State the verdict in one line at the top of the plan.

## Step 3. Email rules

Every cold email must pass all of these.

1. The list is LLM-built against the ICP, never bought. Add a trigger where possible: funding announced, new hire, new location, event attended.
2. Deliverability: dedicated subdomain, warmed for at least 3 weeks, slow ramp. Match providers where possible (Gmail to Gmail, Microsoft to Microsoft).
3. Copy reads like a text. Two sentences. Odd, specific subject line. Lowercase is fine. "Sent from my iPhone" is fine. One typo is fine.
4. Job one is proving a human wrote it. Job two is the reply. No AI-sounding sentences, no dashes, no "I hope this finds you well", no bullet lists, no links in the first email.
5. Written in the founder's own voice. Shorter always wins.
6. Measure reply rate per ICP per week. Below 5% on an untouched ICP means the copy or deliverability is broken. Below 2% on a flooded ICP means stop and change channel.

## Step 4. In person

- Flooded ICP: one in-person motion per month. In order of preference: co-host a small event with an operator who already owns the room (be the only vendor, write the agenda, chair it); a 10-person dinner with a reason to attend beyond being pitched; field presence where customers already gather (trade counters, meetups, demo days).
- Never sponsor when you can co-host. A room that is half vendors is the wrong ratio.
- Events are bottom of funnel for network buyers. Invite people who already know the name.

## Step 5. Lead magnet: comment for a copy

1. Post the offer before the asset exists: "comment X and I'll send you the guide". A bold numeric claim as the hook, real findings in the post, the CTA only at the end.
2. If it travels, write it: audience, goal, table of contents, a goal per section, draft, then read every word. Free for an email.
3. Prioritise assets over posts: books, PDFs, reports. Documents travel further than plain posts on LinkedIn.
4. Physical version for the top 20 accounts: mail the book or printed report with a handwritten note. It beats a dinner.
5. This works in industries that respect subject-matter expertise and are not technologists. It does not work selling into tech. Use product and triggers there.

Reuse assets the founder already owns (a book, a report, a talk) before writing new ones.

## Step 6. AEO: agents are buying

1. Write the 10 questions a buyer, or their agent, would ask that this business should be the answer to.
2. Run them in ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity. Log who gets cited and from which sources.
3. Fix the site: one question, one short direct answer, citable. Cover the complicated edge cases competitors skip, with concrete examples. Anonymised customer work becomes pages.
4. Win one strong authority signal (a trade publication or national press placement) rather than more pages.
5. Ship one free skill or tool the ICP can use, adjacent to the product, with no CTA.
6. Re-run the 10 questions monthly.

Do not recommend account swarms or synthetic query seeding.

## Step 7. Mine the calls

Record every sales call. At 100 calls, run them through a call-mining tool to produce the outbound campaign, the ad copy and the sales script. Customers' own words beat anything written cold.

## Step 8. Tools to name when relevant

ZoomInfo triggers, Instantly for warm-up and sending, a trigger-based LinkedIn DM tool that sends as the founder, a call-mining tool. Name them as options, not requirements.

## Output

One markdown doc per business, under two pages:

- ICP and the flooded or untouched verdict
- Stage verdict and who sells
- The email (two sentences, in the founder's voice) plus the list and trigger definition
- The in-person motion for the next 30 days
- The lead magnet and the comment-for-copy post, drafted
- The 10 AEO questions
- The 30-day plan with weekly reply-rate and meeting targets

Operator tone. Short sentences. No dashes. No theory.

## Verification

- The flooded or untouched verdict is stated, and every channel choice follows from it.
- The cold email is two sentences, human, no dashes, no links, and could have been typed on a phone.
- No VP Sales hire recommended before $10M ARR.
- The lead magnet reuses an existing asset where one exists.
- The AEO section has 10 real questions, not categories.
- The plan has numbers to measure next Friday.
